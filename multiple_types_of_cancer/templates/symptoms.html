Awareness

Common symptoms & how staging works

A short, plain-language primer on warning signs for the four cancer types this project studies, and what "stage" means in general.

BR Brain Cancer

  • Persistent or worsening headaches
  • Seizures with no prior history
  • Blurred or double vision
  • Balance, coordination, or speech difficulty
  • Unexplained memory or personality changes

BC Breast Cancer

  • A new lump or area of thickened tissue
  • Change in breast size, shape, or contour
  • Skin dimpling or puckering
  • Nipple discharge, redness, or inversion
  • Persistent, unexplained breast pain

CX Cervical Cancer

  • Abnormal vaginal bleeding between periods
  • Bleeding after intercourse or menopause
  • Unusual or watery vaginal discharge
  • Pelvic pain or pain during intercourse

LU Lung Cancer

  • A persistent cough that doesn't go away
  • Coughing up blood, even in small amounts
  • Shortness of breath or chest pain
  • Unexplained weight loss and fatigue

These are general warning signs, not a diagnosis. Many of these symptoms overlap with common, non-cancerous conditions - the only way to know for certain is a proper evaluation by a qualified healthcare professional.

Staging

What "stage" means

Most cancers are described using a 0–IV scale that reflects how far the disease has spread - not how severe any single symptom feels.

0

In Situ

Abnormal cells are present but confined to where they first formed.

I

Early Stage

A small, localized tumor that hasn't spread beyond its origin.

II

Regional Growth

The tumor is larger or has reached nearby tissue or lymph nodes.

III

Locally Advanced

Cancer has spread further into surrounding tissue or lymph nodes.

IV

Metastatic

Cancer has spread to distant organs or parts of the body.
